1 · The name

Emilia Glimt

glimt /glimt/: Swedish, Danish and Norwegian for a glint, a flash of light. In Swedish, someone with glimten i ögat has a twinkle in their eye.

It reads like an artist's name, and it describes exactly what the glass does when candlelight hits the gold.

Why it works (the psychology)

A person, not a shop
People trust and root for a face far more than a logo. Leading with "Emilia" means every video, every parcel and every DM builds a relationship with her. That relationship is the brand.
It sounds like a real name
"Emilia Glimt" sounds like a first name and surname, like a painter you'd find at a Stockholm design fair. That's more memorable and more premium than "Emilia Studios" (generic) or "Emilia's Crafts" (cheap).
Easy to process
Two short words, five syllables, no å/ä/ö. Easy to say in English, German, Dutch, French, Italian and Spanish. Easy-to-say names are perceived as more trustworthy and get repeated more, which matters on a platform where people tell friends "look up …".
It means something
Glint and gold, light on glass, a twinkle in the eye. The name carries the product's best feature (gold catching light) and her personality (playful, warm) without saying "glass". So it still fits when friends' ceramics or candles join later.
Nordic, not a cliché
It feels Scandinavian without the worn-out words (hygge, fika, lagom, nord, design, studio). Europeans love "a small maker from Sweden". That's a selling point, so let the name whisper it.
Ownable
On 9 Oct 2026, emiliaglimt.com came back unregistered, and a web search found no business using "Emilia Glimt". It's a distinct search term, so whoever googles it finds her.
Watch-outs (both small):
  • "Glimt" is also the name of the Norwegian football club Bodø/Glimt. It's a different world and might even help recognition in Norway.
  • There's a Swedish design duo called Glimpt (with a P) that has made hand-decorated objects. With "Emilia" in front, the names are clearly different. Still, do a quick search on TMview before printing packaging, and never shorten the brand to just "Glimt".

2 · The IKEA trick

Name products like IKEA does.

The brand is a person. The collections get one short Swedish word each, in capitals. It's simple, collectible and instantly Scandinavian, and every new drop becomes an event ("NATT is gone, DIS drops Friday").

CollectionMeansLook
NATT 01 · nownightBlack edges, gold leaf band. The first design.
DISmistFrosted white with silver flakes, for winter and weddings.
GRYNINGdawnBlush and gold, for spring, Valentine's and Mother's Day.
SANDsandWarm beige and gold, for summer tables.
HAVseaDeep blue with gold, for midsummer and gifts for men.

Rules: one word, ideally 3–5 letters, avoid å/ä/ö in the main word (fine in product names like "NATT Skål"), and always show the meaning ("natt · Swedish for night"). Products inside a collection are named by what they are: NATT Vin (wine), NATT Skål (flute), NATT för två (pair), NATT Bord (table set).

4 · The one idea everything follows

We're selling Emilia.
The glasses come with her.

Anyone can buy a gold-rimmed glass for €8. Nobody else can buy "the glass Emilia painted for me, signed, number 27, with a note in her handwriting." People pay for the story, the person, and being part of her journey early.

Show the person

Her face and voice in most videos. Talking to camera beats silent product shots for building trust.

Show the work

Process proves it's handmade and justifies €38. Mistakes and retakes make it real.

Invite people in

Let followers vote on colours, name collections and see their country on the map. People buy what they helped create.

The website follows the same rule. It opens with "Hej! I'm Emilia", every product has a note from her, checkout asks what she should handwrite, and the thank-you page is a letter from her. That's in the mockup, so check how it feels.

5 · TikTok & Instagram

How to think about going viral

Virality isn't luck. It's a repeatable format plus volume. The platforms show every video to a small test audience first. If people watch to the end, rewatch, share or save, it gets pushed wider. So the job is to make many good bets, not one perfect video.

The 5 rules that matter most

  1. The first 1–2 seconds decide everything. Start mid-action: the brush already on glass, gold already falling. On-screen text states the hook. No "hi guys", no logo intro.
  2. Make it loop. End where it began (empty glass → painted glass → back to the empty glass). Rewatches are a strong signal.
  3. Aim for shares and saves, not likes. "Send this to the friend who…" and "save for Christmas gift ideas" are the actions that spread a video. On Instagram, sends are among the strongest reach signals.
  4. Volume and consistency. 1 TikTok a day for the first 90 days, plus 4–5 Reels a week (mostly the same videos) and Stories daily. Most videos will flop. That's normal. One in twenty carries the account.
  5. Write in English and add subtitles. English reaches all of Europe. Add Swedish words as charm, and sometimes a hook in German, French or Italian to test new markets.

The signature series: Glimt across Europe

Every time an order goes to a new country, she films packing it and adds a gold pin to a big paper map of Europe on her wall. "Order #1 to Portugal 🇵🇹 — 9 countries down, 35 to go."

Why it works: people from that country flood the comments ("Do Austria next!"), which sparks national pride, a clear goal and a running story. It's also a reason to post every day, and it makes Europe-wide shipping visible without ever saying "we ship to Europe".

Turning views into sales

  • Comment-to-DM: "Comment GLIMT and I'll send you the link" (Instagram automation tools like ManyChat). This boosts comments, which boosts reach.
  • Drops, not "always in stock": announce 7 days ahead, use a countdown sticker in Stories, email the waitlist 1 hour early. Limited and numbered makes €38 feel like a steal, not a cost.
  • Instagram broadcast channel ("Emilia's table") for the true fans: drop times, sneak peeks, votes.
  • TikTok Shop is live in some EU countries (e.g. Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Ireland). Check whether she can sell there as a Swedish seller. It's a later step, not a launch must.
  • Answer every DM within a few hours for the first months. Every conversation is a sale or a fan.
  • The website must load fast inside the TikTok/IG browser and offer Apple Pay and Klarna, because most buyers will never type a card number on a phone.

Getting other people to talk about her

  • Micro-creators (5–50k followers) in table-setting, wine, home and wedding content across DE, NL, FR and IT. Gift them a NATT pair with a handwritten note, no strings attached. Expect about 1 in 4 to post.
  • Wine bars and bottle shops in her city. A display glass on the counter with a QR code.
  • Wedding planners and bridal accounts. Bride and groom flutes are a high-value niche: personalised, 4–6 week lead time, €90+ per pair.
  • Always label paid or gifted posts (#ad / "paid partnership"). It's required by law in the EU.

7 · Numbers & routine

A weekly rhythm she can actually keep.

Weekly routine
  • 2 painting sessions filmed from 2 angles (overhead + side). One glass makes about 5 videos.
  • 1 hour of editing in CapCut. Batch 7 videos at once.
  • Daily 20 minutes: post, reply to comments, Stories.
  • Sunday: look at what worked, do more of that.
What to watch (in order)
  1. Shares + saves per video
  2. Watch time / % watched to the end
  3. Waitlist sign-ups
  4. Link clicks → orders (conversion)
  5. Followers (a result, not a goal)

Pricing sanity check

€38 per glass is right for handmade and signed work in Europe. Rule of thumb: price ≥ 4 × (glass + paint + gold + packaging). Her time is the product. Underpricing handmade work makes it look less valuable. Don't discount. Use limited drops, sets ("för två" saves €4) and free EU shipping over €100 to raise order value instead.

8 · Before the first sale

The boring list that protects her.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Skatteverket and verksamt.se have the official answers for Sweden.

  • Food safety: use lead- and cadmium-free glass paint, decorate the outside only, and keep the top ~2 cm (where lips touch) clear. If "black on the edges" means the rim itself, move the black band just below the lip. It's safer and the paint lasts longer. Bake-cure as the paint maker says. Tell buyers: hand wash only.
  • Business: register an enskild firma (sole trader) on verksamt.se and apply for F-skatt. Check the VAT rules with Skatteverket. Once yearly sales to consumers in other EU countries pass €10,000, register for VAT OSS and charge each buyer's country's VAT.
  • EU product safety (GPSR): each product page must show the maker's name, postal and email address, a product identifier, and warnings/care. The mockup's "Product safety" section is the template.
  • 14-day right of withdrawal for EU online buyers on standard items (not for personalised pieces). Put it in the terms. The mockup says this.
  • Glass shipping: box-in-box with 5 cm padding, then drop-test from 1 m before launch. Use tracked and insured shipping (PostNord / DHL / Budbee). Keep the "if it breaks, I repaint it" promise and budget ~3–5% for breakage.
  • No fake reviews or fake scarcity. Both are illegal in the EU. "Only 40" must be true, and so it is a promise to keep.
  • Photos: replace every placeholder in the mockup with real photos of her and the glasses. That's the single biggest upgrade.
